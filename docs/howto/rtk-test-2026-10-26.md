# RTK mountpoint test, 2026-10-26

Test of MaCORS single-station corrections on a fixed mark, about 40 minutes in one session
on the evening of 2026-10-26 local time (EDT), which was 2026-10-27 UTC. It followed
backyard surveys that showed slow height drift while reporting RTK fixed. The network's
iMAX product was not part of this test.

## Data

The first version of this note used the points saved in SW Maps. This revision adds the
receiver's continuous 4 Hz NMEA log from the same session: about 9,000 epochs from
20:30:43 to 21:08:55 EDT (00:30:43–01:08:55 UTC). It has positions, fix status, correction
age, station ID and GST error estimates, but no raw measurements, so it cannot be
reprocessed. Every saved point matches the log at its time to within a few millimetres.

The log was cut into segments where the reference station ID changed or the corrections
dropped out and reconnected. Epochs on stale corrections (age over 5 s) are left out of
the statistics.

## Offsets

Medians of the log segments relative to the `madot_MSM_near` median, in metres. U p95−p5
is the spread of 4 Hz heights. Drift is the range of the 60 s rolling median, so it shows
slow change and ignores short-term scatter. GST σV is the receiver's median reported
vertical sigma. Ratio is the robust standard deviation of U ((p95−p5)/3.29) divided by
GST σV.

## Reading it

- **Drift is confirmed.** On Salisbury (near) the height drifted 8.4 cm over 10 minutes
  while reporting about 1 cm vertical accuracy. It rose about 2 cm and then fell about 7 cm
  over the last 6 minutes. Short-term scatter was only about 0.5 cm, so the error is slow
  drift, and averaging longer at a point does not remove it.
- **The range across all sources is 18–19 cm**, including stations 115–165 km away that
  nobody would use.
- **Station differences and drift can't be separated in one session.** The same Salisbury
  stream read -1.9 cm and +2.9 cm 17 minutes apart. Tewksbury sits 7.7 cm above the first
  Salisbury run but only 2.9 cm above the second. The earlier figure of about 7 cm between
  usable stations does not hold up.
- **Switching sources produced lasting offsets, not short transients.** The height jumped
  +6 cm on the switch to Tewksbury and +10 cm on the switch back to Salisbury, and held
  those levels. Real settling after a switch was 1–3 cm on the usable stations. On the
  115 km station the height moved 17 cm in about 62 s while still reporting fixed.
- **Fixed on stale corrections.** After each switch the receiver kept reporting RTK fixed
  for 5–20 s on corrections from the old stream.
- **Reported accuracy.** GST sigma matched the scatter within a segment, but missed the
  offsets between sources and the drift over minutes.
- **Horizontal** scatter was mostly 1–2.7 cm (95 % radius), and the medians of the usable
  stations agree to within about 2.5 cm.
- **Discarding the first minute after a switch is still sensible**, but it isn't enough on
  long baselines and doesn't remove the offsets.
- The GGA satellite count is capped at 12, so it can't be used to judge geometry; the
  receiver was using about 31 satellites throughout.
- It is a single session. `Ortho Height` is 0 in every saved record, so `Elevation` was
  used. Instrument height is 1.8 m in all layers.

## Implication

Use one correction source for every survey that will be differenced. Changing source can
shift heights by several centimetres, which is large against the 13 cm surface figure from
last season, and even a single source drifts by several centimetres over minutes. Correcting
drift with frequent check shots works but is laborious. A local base in the yard should
remove both the source offsets and most of the drift, and repeating this test on it is how
to confirm that.
